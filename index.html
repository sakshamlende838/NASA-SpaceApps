Bridging Two Decades of Active Fire Hotspots

NASA’s MODIS (1 km) and VIIRS (375 m) sensors observe wildfires at fundamentally different spatial resolutions, swath geometries, and detection thresholds. Directly comparing raw hotspot counts produces artificial trends and misleading wildfire severity. Ignis Track normalizes both sensor streams onto a unified, calibrated scale for emergency responders and scientists.

Harmonization Methodology & Scientific Principles

Scientific Uncertainty Notice: Satellite hotspot detections indicate thermal radiation anomalies during orbital overpasses. They are NOT automatically confirmed ground perimeters, burned area assessments, or structural damage reports.

1. Sensor Differences

MODIS aboard NASA’s Terra and Aqua satellites provides an unbroken historical archive since late 2000 at ~1 km nadir pixel resolution. VIIRS aboard Suomi-NPP (2012+) and NOAA-20/21 (2018+) utilizes a 375 m I-band sensor. Because VIIRS pixels are ~7x smaller in area, VIIRS detects smaller, lower-intensity flaming fronts and breaks single large fire complexes into significantly more detection pixels.

2. The 6-Stage Harmonization Workflow

Stage 1: Traceable Ingestion
Unmutated raw CSV ingestion from NASA FIRMS with complete archive provenance.
Stage 2: Rigorous Cleaning
Filter invalid coordinates, filter non-vegetation fires (type != 0), deduplicate identical points.
Stage 3: Normalized Schema
Standardize confidence: MODIS 0-29 / VIIRS 'l' → low; MODIS 30-79 / VIIRS 'n' → nominal; MODIS 80-100 / VIIRS 'h' → high. Harmonize observation dates to local solar days.
Stage 4: Common Spatial Gridding & Overlap Ratio
Snap detections to a shared 0.01° (~1.1 km) grid. The primary metric is unique Fire-Cell-Days, removing the pixel-area bias. Compute monthly calibration ratio: k_month = median(VIIRS_cells / MODIS_cells) over the 2012–2024 overlap period.
Stage 5: Pre-Aggregation
High-performance pre-aggregated daily and monthly cell summaries.
Stage 6: Robust Z-Score Anomalies
Seasonally-stratified robust z-scores: Z = (x - median) / (1.4826 * MAD) with a minimum 5-year historical baseline guardrail.

3. Satellite Data Status

The calendar strictly separates "0 fires detected" (satellite passed overhead and found no thermal anomalies) from "Missing Data" (satellite orbital gaps, instrument calibration offline, or missing FIRMS telemetry), displayed with diagonal hatched styling.

Ignis Track is an Earth Science data harmonization research platform developed for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge.

Operational Use Disclaimer

This application is provided for informational, historical research, and environmental monitoring purposes only. It is not an emergency dispatch system or immediate tactical evacuation service. Emergency responders and the public must always heed official local emergency notifications and civil protection orders.

Data Accuracy

Thermal anomaly detections are derived from NASA FIRMS active fire datasets. While rigorous scientific harmonization methods have been applied, satellite detections are subject to atmospheric cloud cover, smoke attenuation, and satellite orbit geometry.

Privacy Policy

Ignis Track respects user privacy and complies with modern open-science transparency standards:

  • No Personal Identifiable Information (PII) Stored: We do not collect names, emails, or personal credentials.
  • Local Browser Storage: Filter selections and view preferences are stored exclusively in your local browser session or URL query parameters.
  • Open Data Standards: All exported datasets are completely open, unencumbered, and derived from public NASA Earth Science archives.
